# 1. Agency Operating Manual

## What Shawcliffe Digital Does

We build simple, practical digital systems for local owner-operated businesses: websites, apps,
lead capture, booking, notifications (SMS/email/push), owner dashboards, and QR-based
physical-to-digital tools — plus the local SEO and Google Business Profile work that makes those
things findable. We handle it end to end: sell it, build it, launch it, keep it running.

## Who We Serve

Local, owner-operated, customer-facing businesses that are too small (or too skeptical) for a real
agency but too busy to build this themselves:

- Fruit stands, roadside sellers, farmers-market vendors
- Bakers and food sellers
- Barbers, hairdressers, nail techs, estheticians, massage therapists
- Welders, plumbers, HVAC techs, cleaners, tradespeople
- Small churches, community groups, local service orgs

Common thread: one or two decision-makers, no IT department, they answer their own phone.

## Positioning

"We build the boring-but-essential digital stuff so you can go back to running your business."
Not a branding agency, not a growth-hacking shop, not a dev shop that disappears after launch.
We are the ongoing tech partner for people who don't want to think about tech.

## Values

- **Simple beats impressive.** If the client can't explain it to their spouse, it's too complex.
- **Working beats perfect.** Ship the thing that solves the problem this month, not the platform
  that would be ideal in three years.
- **We answer the phone.** Response time is the actual product for a lot of these clients — they've
  been burned by agencies that vanish.
- **Price like a service, not a project.** One-time builds are how you get paid once; recurring
  support is how the business survives.

## Service Philosophy

We sell outcomes ("more customers know you're open," "you stop answering the same question 20
times a day"), not technology ("a React Native app with push notifications"). The client should
never need to understand the stack. Every project should be maintainable by two people without
a support team — if a tool requires a dedicated ops person to keep alive, don't adopt it.

## Projects We Accept

- Clear, bounded scope: a website, a booking flow, a notification system, a dashboard
- Client who can make a decision (owner or clear delegate, not a committee)
- Realistic budget for the ask (see [pricing](05-proposals-and-pricing.md))
- Client willing to give us content/access within a reasonable window

## Projects We Avoid

- "Build me the next Amazon" — anything requiring a real engineering team and VC-scale ambitions
- Clients who want a fully custom, novel system with no comparable reference (high risk, low reward
  for a 2-person shop)
- No decision-maker access — you're building for a manager who has to relay everything to an absent
  owner
- Can't articulate a budget or timeline and won't engage with discovery — usually a sign they're not
  ready to buy
- Anything requiring us to hold sensitive regulated data we're not equipped to secure (health
  records, full card processing, etc.) — route those to a specialist or a compliant third-party
  processor instead of building it ourselves

## What "Successful Client Project" Means

- Launched on the agreed timeline (or the client agreed to the delay in writing, in advance)
- Client can operate day-to-day without calling us (they can update prices, mark closed, see leads)
- It's actually solving the problem it was sold to solve — leads got captured, calls got reduced,
  whatever the original pain was
- Client is on a support plan by month 2, not chasing us for random free fixes
- We'd be comfortable using them as a reference/case study
