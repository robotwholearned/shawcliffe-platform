# 3. Client Journey & Sales Process

## Client Journey — Full Process

| Stage | What happens | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Lead comes in | Referral, cold outreach reply, form fill, QR scan | Sales | Logged in tracker (see [08](08-admin-legal-metrics-tools.md)) |
| 2. Initial conversation | Quick reply (same day), confirm interest + basic fit | Sales | Booked discovery call or disqualified |
| 3. Qualification | Budget/timeline/authority check | Sales | Go/no-go |
| 4. Discovery call | Structured questions (see [04](04-discovery-templates.md)) | Sales | Notes + recommended package |
| 5. Proposal | Sent within 48 hrs of the call | Sales | Proposal doc (see [05](05-proposals-and-pricing.md)) |
| 6. Contract/payment | Sign + deposit before work starts | Sales/Admin | Signed SOW + deposit received |
| 7. Onboarding | Kickoff, asset/content collection | PM | Filled onboarding checklist ([06](06-project-delivery.md)) |
| 8. Project planning | Confirm scope, timeline, milestones | PM | Shared timeline the client can see |
| 9. Design/build | Actual work | Dev/Design | Working build in staging |
| 10. Internal review | We check it before the client sees it | QA | Punch list resolved |
| 11. Client review | Client sees it, gives feedback | PM | Feedback logged |
| 12. Revisions | Bounded to what's in scope | Dev | Updated build |
| 13. Launch | Goes live, see [launch checklist](06-project-delivery.md) | PM/Dev | Live product |
| 14. Training | Client learns to use their own dashboard | PM | Client comfortable, no follow-up panic |
| 15. Monthly support | Ongoing relationship starts | Support | Recurring revenue ([07](07-client-training-and-support.md)) |
| 16. Upsells/retention | Check in, suggest next thing when relevant | Sales | Expanded scope or renewal |

## Lead Sources

- Referrals from existing clients (best source — always ask happy clients for one name)
- Local Facebook groups / community boards
- Driving around and cold-walking into shops (works surprisingly well for this client type)
- QR code business cards handed out in person
- Google Business Profile / local search once you have a couple live sites
- Church/community network (word of mouth travels fast in these circles)

## Cold Outreach

Walk in or call, lead with a specific observation ("I noticed you don't have a way for people to
see if you're open today"), not a pitch. Goal of first contact is just a second conversation, not a
sale. See script in [09-templates-and-scripts.md](09-templates-and-scripts.md).

## Warm Outreach

Referrals and past clients — mention who referred them immediately, skip straight to "what's your
biggest headache right now."

## Referral Tracking

One column in the lead tracker: "Referred by." Say thank you every time (a card, a discount on their
next month, whatever) — referrals are your cheapest acquisition channel and drying up thank-yous
kills them.

## Discovery Call Questions

Use the type-specific questionnaire in [04-discovery-templates.md](04-discovery-templates.md).
Universal starting question for any call: **"Walk me through what happens when a new customer tries
to reach you today."** Everything else branches from their answer.

## Qualification Criteria

| Signal | Good | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Decision authority | Owner or clear delegate on the call | "I'll have to ask my partner/board/manager" repeatedly |
| Budget | Can name a rough number or range | Refuses to discuss money before seeing "the full proposal" |
| Timeline | Has a real reason to move (season, event, pain) | "Just looking around," no urgency |
| Problem clarity | Can describe the actual pain | Wants "a website" with no reason why |
| Responsiveness | Replies within a day or two | Ghosts between initial contact and call |

## Red Flags (When to Slow Down or Walk Away)

- Wants a full build before paying anything ("build it and I'll see if I like it")
- Repeatedly renegotiates scope before the contract is even signed
- Asks you to skip a deposit "because we've been talking a while"
- Compares you to a $200 template site and won't discuss why the price differs
- Rude to you or your team on the discovery call — it gets worse after they've paid, not better
- Wants something outside what we accept (see [01](01-operating-manual.md))

**When to walk away:** any two red flags together, or one red flag plus a gut check that says no.
It's better to lose one bad-fit lead than spend three months on a client who never pays on time.

## How to Explain Our Services Simply

Never lead with the tech. Lead with the outcome:

- Website → "A place people find you and trust you before they call."
- Lead capture → "You stop losing customers who almost reached out but didn't."
- Booking system → "People book themselves instead of playing phone tag."
- Notifications → "Your customers know the second you're open or back in stock."
- Dashboard → "You update your own prices and hours from your phone, no calling us."

## Selling Value Without Being Pushy

- Ask about their pain before mentioning any feature — the pitch should sound like a response to
  what they just told you, not a script.
- Quantify the cost of the status quo in their own words ("so you said you lose about 3 calls a
  week to voicemail — what's an average sale worth?").
- Offer the smallest package that solves the actual pain, not the biggest one you sell. Trust
  compounds; upsells come later ([07](07-client-training-and-support.md)).

## Handling Price Objections

1. Don't drop the price first — ask what specifically feels off (the number, the payment terms, or
   whether they see the value yet).
2. Reframe against their alternative: DIY time cost, a competitor's monthly ad spend, or what one
   missed customer/week costs them.
3. If budget is genuinely tight, shrink scope, don't shrink price per feature — offer the smaller
   package instead of discounting the big one.
4. Never discount without removing something. A "discount" that doesn't shrink scope trains every
   future client to expect the same.

## Following Up Professionally

- After discovery call, no proposal: follow up in 2 business days.
- After proposal sent, no response: follow up at day 3, day 7, then day 14 (see templates).
- Three follow-ups with no response = move to a quarterly "just checking in" cadence, stop chasing
  weekly. Respect their time and yours.

## When to Walk Away From a Lead

Two unresolved red flags, a client who wants to skip the contract, or a scope that doesn't match
what we accept ([01](01-operating-manual.md)). Say so plainly and refer them elsewhere if you can —
it's better for the relationship and your reputation than a project that goes sideways.
